# Complete Feature Catalog

Each item states the training method and why it exists.

## A. Pitch, interval, and tonal-center hearing
1. **Scale-degree recognition** - establish a tonic, play one pitch, identify degree including chromatic alterations. Builds functional relative pitch.
2. **Scale-degree production** - establish tonic and request a sung/played degree. Tests internal generation rather than recognition.
3. **Absolute note recognition (optional)** - identify note names without tonal context. Useful but secondary to functional hearing.
4. **Melodic interval recognition** - ascending/descending intervals over varied timbres/registers. Builds melodic distance perception.
5. **Harmonic interval recognition** - simultaneous interval identification. Supports chord decomposition.
6. **Interval production** - sing/play a requested interval from a given pitch. Stronger evidence than multiple choice.
7. **Tonal-center identification** - hear a phrase and identify tonic/key center. Necessary for functional transcription.
8. **Mode recognition** - identify modes through characteristic degrees, not mood labels. Includes major/minor modes and later melodic-minor families.

## B. Chord vocabulary
9. **Basic triad quality** - major/minor/diminished/augmented/sus2/sus4/power chord.
10. **Seventh-chord quality** - maj7, 7, m7, mMaj7, half-diminished, diminished 7, augmented variants.
11. **Extensions** - 6, 9, maj9, m9, 11, 13, add9 and related colors.
12. **Altered dominants** - b9, #9, b5/#11, #5/b13, combinations.
13. **Chord decomposition** - ask separately for root, third, seventh, extensions, alterations, bass.
14. **Chord spelling after hearing** - identify component pitches after naming the harmony.
15. **Inversion recognition** - identify root/1st/2nd/3rd inversion.
16. **Bass-note recognition** - identify only the sounding bass beneath a chord.
17. **Slash-chord recognition** - independently identify upper harmony and bass note.
18. **Arpeggiated chord recognition** - same harmony presented sequentially.
19. **Short-duration chord recognition** - decreasing exposure time to build instant recognition.
20. **Timbre-generalized chord recognition** - same concepts across piano, Rhodes, guitar, organ, strings, pads, ensembles.
21. **Register-generalized recognition** - low/mid/high and mixed spacing.
22. **Rhythm-generalized recognition** - chords embedded in varied rhythmic patterns.

## C. Voicings and voice leading
23. **Close/open position recognition** - distinguish spacing while preserving chord identity.
24. **Drop-2/drop-3 exposure** - hear and reproduce common arranging voicings.
25. **Shell voicings** - root/3rd/7th and rootless variants.
26. **Rootless jazz voicings** - recognize and reproduce function without root present.
27. **Quartal voicings** - identify characteristic fourth-based structures.
28. **Spread triads** - hear widely spaced triadic structures.
29. **Upper-structure triads** - hear altered dominant colors as upper structures.
30. **Exact voicing copy** - hear a voicing and reproduce exact notes/octaves with MIDI/on-screen keyboard.
31. **Voicing-equivalence mode** - early grading may accept functionally equivalent arrangements; advanced mode requires exact replication.
32. **Guide-tone hearing** - isolate and track 3rds/7ths through progressions.
33. **Voice-leading tracking** - identify which voice moved, interval/direction, and common tones.
34. **Inner-voice melody recognition** - determine whether melody is soprano/alto/tenor/bass and reproduce it.
35. **What-changed comparison** - A/B near-identical voicings; identify altered note, bass, extension, inversion, or substitution.

## D. Functional harmony
36. **Function classification** - tonic/predominant/dominant first; exact symbol later.
37. **Roman numeral recognition** - identify harmony relative to key.
38. **Progression chunk recognition** - ii-V-I, I-vi-ii-V, I-V-vi-IV, blues, rhythm changes, circle progressions, etc.
39. **Cadence recognition** - authentic, half, plagal, deceptive, backdoor, modal, substitute resolutions.
40. **Secondary dominant recognition** - identify target and V/x function.
41. **Modal interchange** - identify borrowed iv, bVI, bVII, bIII, iiø, etc.
42. **Tritone substitution** - distinguish V7-I from bII7-I and hear substitutions in context.
43. **Diminished passing harmony** - common-tone and dominant diminished functions.
44. **Backdoor dominant recognition** - identify iv/bVII7-related dominant motion where applicable.
45. **Chromatic mediants** - recognize non-diatonic third-related shifts.
46. **Pedal point hearing** - separate changing upper harmony from sustained bass.
47. **Modulation detection** - detect point of key-center change and identify destination.
48. **Pivot-chord analysis by ear** - identify common/pivot function where meaningful.

## E. Melody, bass, and transcription
49. **Melody echo** - hear 2-4 notes, reproduce; scale to longer phrases.
50. **Melody transcription** - enter heard melody via keyboard, MIDI, notation, fretboard, or solfege/scale degree.
51. **Bass echo/transcription** - bass-only first, then full mix.
52. **Two-bar chord transcription** - enter chord symbols/Roman numerals.
53. **4/8/16-bar progression transcription** - increasingly long harmonic memory and analysis.
54. **Full-song transcription lab** - imported audio, region looping, speed change without pitch change, waveform/beat grid.
55. **Beat-aligned change detection** - mark exactly when harmony changes.
56. **Stems ladder** - bass only -> harmony -> melody -> drums -> full mix.
57. **Noise/masking ladder** - solo source -> accompaniment -> dense mix.
58. **Real-music benchmark** - previously unseen excerpts scored separately from synthetic practice.
59. **Guided hints** - reveal key -> bass -> function -> chord family -> exact answer only as needed.
60. **Ambiguity-aware transcription** - permit multiple defensible labels where analysis is genuinely non-unique.

## F. Harmonization and creative hearing
61. **Melody-over-chord recognition** - identify harmony under a melody note/phrase.
62. **Harmonization exercise** - choose/play plausible chords beneath a melody; allow multiple correct solutions.
63. **Reharmonization exercise** - transform a basic progression using substitutions, mixture, diminished passing chords, backdoor/tritone approaches, etc.
64. **Compare harmonizations** - hear original and alternatives and identify functional/voice-leading differences.

## G. Singing and performance
65. **Chord-tone singing** - sing root/3rd/5th/7th/extensions from a sounded chord/root.
66. **Guide-tone singing through progressions** - sing 3rds/7ths across harmonic motion.
67. **Root/bass singing** - sing root motion or actual bass line.
68. **Call-and-response** - app plays melody, bass phrase, chord or voicing; user reproduces.
69. **MIDI piano performance mode** - hear request then play chord/function/voicing.
70. **Guitar performance mode** - reproduce through microphone/MIDI/fretboard input.
71. **Functional performance prompts** - e.g., "play a rootless ii-V-I in Eb" or "play the 7th of V7".

## H. Memory and automaticity
72. **Delayed comparison** - hear chord A, wait, hear B, identify change.
73. **Multi-bar memory** - hear progression once and reconstruct without unlimited replay.
74. **Reaction-time training** - gradually shorten response window without sacrificing accuracy.
75. **Blind/audio-only mode** - remove keyboard graphics, notation, chord spelling, visual hints.
76. **Post-answer visualization** - after judgment, show keyboard/fretboard/notation and highlight defining tones.

## I. Adaptive learning
77. **Adaptive difficulty engine** - independently adjust supported difficulty dimensions.
78. **Confusion-matrix training** - target exact recurring confusions.
79. **Contrast drills** - intensive A/B discrimination between confused concepts.
80. **Error replay** - immediate comparison, isolate defining tones, then generate a fresh near-transfer example.
81. **Spaced repetition** - schedule delayed checks based on evidence of retention.
82. **Interleaving** - mix learned categories so exercise type is not predictable.
83. **Mastery evidence** - require consistency across days, keys, timbres, registers and contexts.
84. **Confidence calibration** - optional user confidence with calibration tracking.
85. **Personal curriculum generator** - daily plan generated from weaknesses, retention, goals and transfer needs.
86. **Diagnostic/recalibration test** - broad placement assessment that can skip known material.

## J. Genre/application profiles
87. **Jazz curriculum** - ii-V-I, altered dominants, diminished harmony, guide tones, rootless voicings, substitutions, extensions.
88. **Modern pop/K-pop curriculum** - inversions, slash chords, modal mixture, suspended/add9 harmony, pedals, chromatic mediants, deceptive motion.
89. **R&B/neo-soul curriculum** - extended chords, upper structures, chromatic voice leading, dense voicings.
90. **Rock/guitar curriculum** - power chords, riffs, pedal tones, modal harmony, guitar voicings.
91. **Classical/tonal curriculum** - functional cadences, sequences, inversions, modulation, part tracking.

## K. Progress and study infrastructure
92. **Automatic session log** - what was trained, duration, attempts, difficulty, errors, gains.
93. **Skill history** - rolling evidence for every skill.
94. **Then-vs-now comparison** - compare current state to prior dates.
95. **Weakness list** - current bottlenecks with evidence.
96. **Capability milestones** - record first time a behavior becomes reliably demonstrated.
97. **Manual factual note** - optional short observation, not a diary requirement.
98. **Practice planning** - recommended next work based on evidence, with manual override.
99. **Export** - CSV/JSON/PDF or shareable report of progress and history.
100. **Backup/restore** - local export and optional sync.
